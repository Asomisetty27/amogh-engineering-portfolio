The problem

Datacenters alarm on GPU temperature, but temperature mostly reflects how hard a GPU is working. A GPU whose cooling is degrading can sit under a light job and look normal until it throttles or fails.

What I built

  • A peer-relative detector: effective thermal resistance Rθ = ΔT / P, computed by two-way median polish across position- and power-matched peers, with a permutation-null test gating every claim before a signal counts.
  • A signature-matrix fault classifier (6-axis fingerprint, 3-valued evidence model) that reproduces all three Princeton attributions.
  • Cross-vendor collectors behind one hardware layer: NVIDIA NVML and DCGM, AMD amdsmi, Redfish BMC.
  • Production packaging: pip install runtheta, a Helm chart and a Go controller that writes gpu-health node labels, over 500 tests in CI across Python 3.10 to 3.12.

What I learned

Stage 1 ran on a free Colab Tesla T4 (n = 7): a 2 °C ambient change drove a 3.5× change in power-recovery time. The most important later result was a flaw I found in my own method. On a rented 8×A100 node, baseboard position alone moved healthy readings 52% at matched power. Rebuilding the detector around position is what made the Princeton result hold up.
